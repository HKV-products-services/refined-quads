Water areas

Water areas are where the 1D network carries the flow, so no 2D cells are kept there. Typically you refine along a watercourse and then cut the watercourse out: the small cells that remain follow its edge as closely as possible.

Cut out cell by cell

generate first makes the whole mesh and then removes cells with MeshKernel, exactly as the model-building workflow does (hydrolib’s mesh2d_clip): the water polygons are merged, each part is clipped with MeshKernel’s mesh deletion, and hanging edges are removed. The water removal rule decides which cells go:

Rule A cell is removed when MeshKernel option
"centre" (default) its circumcentre (triangle) or centroid lies in water FACES_WITH_INCLUDED_CIRCUMCENTERS
"inside" it lies completely in water INSIDE_NOT_INTERSECTED
"touches" it touches water INSIDE_AND_INTERSECTED 
meshes = {
    rule: generate(
        domain,
        cell_size=16.0,
        refinement_areas=[(river.buffer(10), 2)],
        transition_rows=1,
        water_areas=[river],
        water_removal=rule,
        crs="EPSG:28992",
    )
    for rule in ("centre", "inside", "touches")
}
NoteWhat MeshKernel does

MeshKernel keeps nodes and edges and derives the cells from them again. A single removed cell whose edges all stay (its neighbours all remain) therefore comes back, and a hole of three or four nodes becomes a cell again. That is accepted here, because it is what the models are built with; give bed levels as samples that are interpolated onto the cells rather than as values per cell.

Small water areas stay cells

A water area of one or two cells would leave a small hole: a ring of at most six nodes. Every program that builds the cells from nodes and edges with MeshKernel — hydrolib’s mesh2d_from_netcdf, the D-HYDRO GUI — makes one cell of such a hole, often a hexagon that D-Flow FM rejects as “network is not orthogonal”. generate and refine therefore keep the cells of such a hole: they stay ordinary lattice cells, so every program sees the same mesh (see ADR-0004). Larger water areas leave larger holes, which stay open. check reports holes that MeshKernel would close, also in meshes from other programs.

Holes in the domain

A hole in the domain is clipped the same way, with the same rule. An island inside a hole keeps its cells.

In the TOML file

water_removal = "centre"

[[water_areas]]
